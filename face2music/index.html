Overview / 01

Turning natural facial movement into dynamic musical patterns.

Facial tracking to sound

Face2Music is a face-controlled sequencer powered by FaceOSC, a standalone desktop program that tracks facial features through a camera and transmits them over Open Sound Control (OSC).

The horizontal position of the face modulates note brightness, vertical position influences resonance, distance from the camera adjusts the filter cutoff, and mouth height determines sustain. Together, these controls let users shape the sound through natural movements.

The patch uses a randomized sequencer with an eight-note pool, reshuffled every fourth repetition to generate evolving musical patterns. A looping module also supports recording, playback, and pausing across three independent tracks.

How to use

Follow these steps to explore the instrument and create your own patterns.

  1. Download and run FaceOSC.exe.
  2. Turn on the camera input.
  3. Adjust the sequencer and press Start.
  4. Move your face to explore different timbres.
  5. Record and experiment with the three available loops.
  6. Combine your sounds and enjoy the result.

Motivation / 03

Lowering barriers
to creation.

Why this project exists

Making music production accessible to beginners has long been one of my academic interests. This passion began while volunteering as a music teacher for children at a local community center.

Many of the children, aged seven to eight, struggled to connect with music and recognize its universality and possibilities. I understood that feeling: when I was younger, my own exposure to music was limited to classical and pop. Hearing Guns N' Roses' “Welcome to the Jungle” showed me how diverse and powerful music could be.

Face2Music grew from the desire to make that sense of discovery more accessible. By turning familiar physical movements into musical controls, the project encourages children and beginners to experiment, listen, and create without first needing to master a traditional instrument.

Engineering / 04

Technical
implementation.

System architecture

The project integrates computer vision tracking via FaceOSC with real-time digital signal processing inside Max/MSP, combining custom OSC mapping, algorithmic sequencing, and physical modeling synthesis.

01

Facial tracking & mapping

FaceOSC sends facial tracking data through OSC. I receive these messages in Max/MSP, scale values to usable ranges, and store them in variables for intuitive sound control.

02

Randomized sequencing

The sequencer uses a multislider to store note values and zl.scramble to reshuffle them every fourth repetition triggered by a 500ms metro, converted to MIDI for synthesis.

03

Karplus–Strong synthesis

Synthesis begins with a random noise buffer generated by rand~ 600, shaped by low-pass filtering and feedback to simulate the decay of a plucked string.

04

Looping & playback

Outputs route to three independent tracks using buffer~, record~, and groove~ objects with clocker coordination for precise recording and playback control.
