Forbes Avenue

Tags: Original, Midwest Emo, Sampling, Guitar

I composed this midwest emo instrumental song during my first month at Carnegie Mellon University, a period of transition from the bustling streets of Shanghai to the more subdued atmosphere of Pittsburgh. This was also an opportunity for me to live on my own and to come to recognize who I am, what I like, and what kind of life is best for me. The piece was an attempt to capture the tranquility of everyday life at Pittsburgh. Its sound reflects the introspective nature of the transition, expressing solitude, nostalgia, and quiet contentment.

I recorded 10 guitar tracks using my Fender Telecaster Deluxe, which I brought with me, and used Neural DSP Cory Wong Archetype plugin to create a warm tone. I EQ'd the guitars to boost the low-mid frequencies and applied compression when needed. I doubled guitars and panned the tracks 100% to the left and right in the chorus to create a wall of sound, enhancing the stereo image and contrasting with the more intimate passages of the arrangement. I sent guitar tracks to a shared reverb bus in various dry/wet ratio to glue them together while being aware to not make them sound too muddy because mid frequencies will build up. I also reversed a guitar recording to create an atmospheric texture that adds movement to the arrangement.

To further ground the piece in its setting, I incorporated a sample of the pedestrian traffic warning, “Wait, wait to cross Morewood Avenue at Forbes Avenue.” This familiar urban announcement introduces a subtle element of environmental sound, anchoring the instrumental in the everyday experience of living in Pittsburgh. By placing a recognizable fragment of the city's soundscape alongside the layered guitars, I aimed to blur the boundary between music and the surrounding environment, capturing not only the quietness of my new home but also the small, ordinary details through which a place gradually becomes familiar.
